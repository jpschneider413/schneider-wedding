Spain is a beautiful country with many worthwhile tourist destinations. Here we provide some travel recommendations (in Barcelona as well as throughout Spain) to help you get the most out of your trip.

Gaudí & Architecture

Sagrada Família

No trip to Barcelona is complete without seeing the Sagrada Família, Antoni Gaudí's extraordinary basilica and the city's most famous landmark. The interior is absolutely worth visiting, but tickets often sell out, so we strongly recommend booking in advance.

Tickets & Information

Park Güell

Another of Gaudí's masterpieces, Park Güell combines colorful architecture, gardens, and some excellent views over Barcelona. Advance tickets are recommended.

Tickets & Information

Casa Batlló

One of Gaudí's two iconic buildings on Passeig de Gràcia, Casa Batlló is known for its colorful façade, organic shapes, and imaginative interior. It is one of the best places in Barcelona to experience Gaudí's architecture up close.

Website

La Pedrera / Casa Milà

Just a short walk from Casa Batlló, La Pedrera (Casa Milà) is another of Gaudí's most famous works. The building is particularly known for its unusual stone façade and spectacular rooftop.

Website

Museums & Culture

Picasso Museum

Located in Barcelona's historic El Born neighborhood, the Picasso Museum houses an extensive collection of Pablo Picasso's work, with a particular focus on his formative years and his connection to Barcelona.

Tickets & Information

Museu Nacional d'Art de Catalunya

The National Art Museum of Catalonia (MNAC) is housed in the impressive Palau Nacional on Montjuïc. In addition to its extensive collection of Catalan art, the museum and surrounding area offer excellent views over Barcelona.

Tickets & Information

Moco Museum Barcelona

For something more contemporary, the Moco Museum features modern and contemporary art in a historic palace in El Born.

Website

Poble Espanyol

Built for the 1929 Barcelona International Exposition, Poble Espanyol is an open-air architectural museum on Montjuïc that recreates streets, squares, and buildings from different regions of Spain. It is an easy way to experience a little of the country beyond Barcelona without leaving the city.

Tickets & Information

FC Barcelona Museum

Football fans can visit the FC Barcelona Museum to explore the history of Barça, its players, trophies, and connection to the city.

Tickets & Information

Experiences

Flamenco

Although flamenco originated in southern Spain rather than Catalonia, Barcelona has several excellent places to see it performed live.

Palau Dalmases offers flamenco performances inside a beautiful historic palace in the Gothic / El Born area.

Palau Dalmases

Flamenco Laietana is another option for an intimate live flamenco performance in central Barcelona.

Flamenco Laietana

Sunset Catamaran Cruise

For a different view of Barcelona, take a sunset catamaran cruise along the Mediterranean, with cava and live music on board. It is an especially nice option for an evening before dinner.

Cruise Information

Helicopter Flight over Barcelona

See Barcelona and its coastline from above with a helicopter flight over the city.

More Information

Barcelona Bus Turístic

If you want an easy way to see many of Barcelona's major sights, the hop-on, hop-off Barcelona Bus Turístic operates several routes around the city. Choose the blue or red route depending on which attractions you want to visit.

Routes & Tickets

Food & Drinks

El Nacional

One of Barcelona's best-known dining destinations, El Nacional occupies a spectacular converted industrial building just off Passeig de Gràcia. Inside are several restaurants and bars specializing in different styles of Spanish cuisine. It is particularly convenient if you are already visiting Casa Batlló or La Pedrera.

Website

Sips

Barcelona is one of the world's great cocktail cities. Sips was named the World's Best Bar in 2023 and is known for inventive, highly creative cocktails.

Website

Paradiso

Another internationally famous Barcelona cocktail bar, Paradiso was named the World's Best Bar in 2022. Hidden behind the entrance of a pastrami shop in El Born, it specializes in elaborate and unconventional cocktails.

Website

La Dolce Vitae

For drinks with a view, La Dolce Vitae is the rooftop terrace of the Majestic Hotel on Passeig de Gràcia. It has panoramic views over central Barcelona and is a particularly nice spot around sunset.

Website

Exploring Beyond Barcelona

If you are turning the wedding into a longer trip, Spain has an enormous amount to explore beyond Barcelona. These are some recommendations for extending your visit.

Andalucía

For warmer weather, historic cities, excellent food, and some of Spain's most distinctive architecture, Andalucía is one of the best regions to explore.

Seville

The region's largest historic city, famous for its cathedral, Alcázar, beautiful plazas, tapas, and flamenco culture.

Granada

Home to the spectacular Alhambra, one of Spain's most important examples of Islamic architecture.

Córdoba

Best known for the Mezquita-Catedral, an extraordinary building reflecting the city's Islamic and Christian history.

Málaga

A Mediterranean city combining a historic center, museums, restaurants, and easy access to the coast.

Cádiz

One of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in Western Europe, with a compact historic center almost entirely surrounded by the Atlantic.

Las Alpujarras

For nature and mountain scenery, consider Las Alpujarras, a region of whitewashed villages on the southern slopes of the Sierra Nevada.

Extremadura

For somewhere quieter and less heavily visited, consider Extremadura in southwestern Spain. The region combines Roman ruins, medieval towns, countryside, and excellent food.

Mérida

Particularly notable for its remarkably preserved Roman heritage, including its Roman theater and amphitheater.

Cáceres

Home to one of Spain's best-preserved medieval and Renaissance old towns.

Trujillo

A beautiful historic town known for its castle, medieval center, and grand Plaza Mayor.

Other towns worth exploring include Hervás, Alburquerque, and Zafra. Extremadura is particularly well suited to a road trip.

Historic Central & Northern Spain

Spain's interior is filled with beautiful medieval cities. Some particularly worthwhile destinations include Segovia, Ávila, Salamanca, Toledo, Burgos, León, and Zamora.

Segovia

Famous for its enormous Roman aqueduct, historic old town, and fairy-tale Alcázar of Segovia.

Ávila

Best known for its remarkably intact medieval walls, which completely surround the historic center.

Salamanca

A beautiful university city known for its monumental sandstone architecture, historic university, and Plaza Mayor.

Toledo

One of Spain's great historic cities, with an extraordinary mixture of Christian, Jewish, and Islamic heritage.

Farther north, Burgos, León, and Zamora offer medieval cathedrals, Romanesque architecture, and historic old towns.

Keep in mind that inland Spain can be considerably colder than Barcelona and Andalucía during the winter.

Canary Islands

For dramatic landscapes and warmer weather, the Canary Islands are another excellent option.

Tenerife

The largest of the Canary Islands, offering everything from beaches and historic towns to the volcanic landscapes surrounding Mount Teide.

Lanzarote

Known for its extraordinary desert-like volcanic landscape and unique vineyards, where grapes are grown in volcanic soil.

La Palma

Much greener than Lanzarote and particularly well suited to hiking, nature, and dramatic mountain scenery.

Fuerteventura & El Hierro

For something quieter and more remote, Fuerteventura is known for its beaches and arid landscapes, while El Hierro is a small, rugged island particularly suited to travelers looking for nature and fewer crowds.
